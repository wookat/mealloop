# MealLoop — naming & tone of voice

## Product name
- Always **MealLoop** — one word, capital M and L. Never "Mealloop", "Meal Loop", "mealloop" (except in URLs/handles: `mealloop`).
- Domain: mealloop.zalize.com. Email: mealloop@zalize.com. Sender name: `MealLoop <mealloop@zalize.com>`.
- Company/controller line for legal & press: "MealLoop (Zalize)".

## Tone of voice
Warm, plain, unhurried. Like a competent friend who cooks — not a chef, not a coach, not a bro.

1. **Plain words.** "Add a recipe", not "Ingest content". Grade-6 reading level.
2. **You/we.** Talk to the reader ("your week", "your list"); "we" only for things we did ("We never sell your data").
3. **Short sentences, active voice.** Cut every word that doesn't help.
4. **Honest, never hyped.** No "revolutionary", "supercharge", "10x", "unleash". Say what it does.
5. **Calm in errors.** Blame the situation, offer the next step: "The AI couldn't draft a week just now — try again in a moment, or use Fill empty dinners."
6. **Light warmth, no jokes in flows.** One emoji max, only in celebratory/marketing contexts (📬 ✨); never in errors or legal.
7. **Food-positive, judgment-free.** Never moralize about eating ("guilt-free", "cheat day", "clean eating" are banned).

## Word list
- Prefer: dinner, week, family, household, share, sync, aisle, plan → shop → cook.
- Beta wording (fixed): "in open beta — all features free during the beta". Never plain "free product" or "100% free forever".
- Banned: guilt-free, cheat, clean eating, hack (as noun), revolutionary, disrupt, AI-powered (as headline adjective), "users" in UI copy (say "your household" / "you").

## Microcopy patterns
- Buttons: verb-first, 1–4 words ("Add week's ingredients", "Start cooking", "Open the planner").
- Empty states: one sentence of what goes here + a CTA (never a dead end).
- Success: state the result, not praise ("Added 12 new items", not "Awesome!").
- Emails: subject ≤ 55 chars, body plain-text-first, always an unsubscribe line on non-transactional mail.
